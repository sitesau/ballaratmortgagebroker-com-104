Potential Costs You Might Face

While you might not pay the broker, there are other costs associated with taking out a loan. The most common additional cost is lenders mortgage insurance, or LMI. This applies if your deposit is less than 20 per cent of the property value. The source material highlights that low deposit options often involve LMI, which protects the lender rather than you. Understanding this cost is crucial when budgeting for a first home or investment property. For first home buyers, schemes like the First Home Guarantee or the Regional First Home Buyer Guarantee can help reduce the need for LMI, but eligibility thresholds apply.

Refinancing can also incur costs. You may face discharge fees from your current lender and valuation fees to determine the property's current worth. The source material suggests that a Ballarat refinance broker can model these numbers before you switch. They can flag any discharge or valuation costs upfront so you know exactly how much you will save or lose by refinancing. It is important to look at the total cost of the loan, not just the headline interest rate. A lower rate might come with higher fees, negating any savings.

For investment loans, the cost structure can be more complex. The source material mentions comparison rate, interest-only periods and tax-aware setup. These factors can affect your cash flow and the overall return on investment. A broker can help structure investment loans with the right comparison rate and interest-only periods to suit your financial strategy. They can also assist with construction and renovation loans, which have specific timing and progress-payment requirements.
